Repiping in Caldwell, ID

When repeated repairs add up to a repipe

Repiping in Caldwell is for pinhole leaks that keep coming back, rusty water and pressure that drops at every tap. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because the same pipe splitting twice usually means more of it is worn.

Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__Jump to the checks

What you may notice

  • Pinhole leaks that keep appearing at different spots
  • Brown or rusty water when a tap first opens
  • Falling pressure at several fixtures
  • Green or white crust on pipes and joints
  • Water stains in a wall that return after a repair

What is often behind it

  • Age and corrosion in older metal supply pipe
  • Mineral scale narrowing the inside of a pipe
  • Water chemistry that attacks metal plumbing over time
  • Damage from a past freeze that weakened a section
  • Original materials, such as lead pipe or lead solder in older homes, that owners want to remove

Checks you can make yourself

Stick to checks that feel easy and safe. Never open a water heater, relight a pilot or touch gas piping or wiring; call instead.

  • Record where and when each leak appeared. A written pattern helps a technician judge whether one pipe is failing or the whole system is.
  • Run a cold tap until the water clears and note whether it is brown or rusty at first.
  • Look at the visible pipe near the main shutoff and water heater and note its color and material: reddish copper, gray steel, or plastic.
  • In Caldwell, look up your address on the city's lead service line inventory, which the 2025 water quality report links.

Stop and phone if

  • A pipe is leaking and you cannot reach a shutoff valve
  • Water appears suddenly inside a wall or ceiling
  • The same pipe has split more than once
  • Water pressure drops sharply across the whole house

Questions about repiping

How urgent is a repipe?
Begin with a quick 1-to-10 gut check. Up at 8–10, with a leak you cannot shut off, water inside a wall or pressure that collapses house-wide, close the main shutoff if you can and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ now. In the middle (4–7), such as pinholes in different spots or rusty water at first draw, call this week, because worn pipe fails again. Down at 1–3, where just one fixture has low flow, look at the aerator first.
How do I know it is time to repipe?
Repeated leaks in different places, rusty water at the tap and falling pressure at several fixtures are the usual signals. A written leak history helps a plumber judge whether spot repairs are still the sensible choice.
Does repiping need a permit?
Idaho Code 54-2620 requires a permit to install, extend or alter a plumbing system, and the state permit worksheet lists a water re-pipe line. Caldwell, Nampa and Middleton run their own plumbing permit offices, so ask yours who issues it and which inspections follow.
Does my house have lead plumbing?
The EPA ties lead pipes mostly to older cities and to houses that predate 1986. Caldwell publishes a lead service line inventory and reported no lead detected in its 2023 sampling. For any town, ask your water utility what it knows about the line to your address.
