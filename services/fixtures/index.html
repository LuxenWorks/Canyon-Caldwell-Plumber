Toilet and Faucet Repair in Caldwell, ID

Everyday repairs for toilets, faucets and disposals

Toilet and faucet repair in Caldwell handles the running toilet, the dripping faucet and the humming disposal. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because a small fix now costs less than a leak under the sink later.

What you may notice

  • A toilet that runs after flushing or refills on its own
  • A faucet that drips, sprays sideways or has weak flow
  • A garbage disposal that hums but will not spin
  • A toilet that rocks, or has water around its base
  • A handle or valve that is stiff, loose or will not shut off fully

What is often behind it

  • A worn flapper, the rubber seal at the bottom of the toilet tank
  • A tired cartridge, rubber washer or O-ring inside the faucet body
  • Mineral flakes clogging an aerator, the small screen at the faucet tip
  • Food waste or a foreign object jammed against the disposal's grinding plate
  • A failed wax ring, the seal between a toilet and the floor flange

Checks you can make yourself

Stick to checks that feel easy and safe. Never open a water heater, relight a pilot or touch gas piping or wiring; call instead.

  • Put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and wait 10 minutes. The EPA says color in the bowl means a leak, and most toilet leaks come from worn flappers.
  • Unscrew a faucet aerator by hand and rinse out grit or mineral flakes.
  • Look at supply lines under sinks and behind toilets for bulges, rust or crusty buildup.
  • Keep your hands out of a disposal. If it hums without turning, switch it off at the wall switch and call.

Stop and phone if

  • A supply line is spraying or a shutoff valve will not close. Close the main shutoff and call.
  • Water is leaking through the ceiling below a bathroom or kitchen.
  • A disposal smokes, sparks or trips a breaker. Leave it off and call.
  • A toilet bowl or tank is cracked and leaking.

Idaho and city details

  • Rules on swapping fixtures differ by city. Nampa's plumbing permit page exempts broken-fixture replacement. Middleton's code lists "repairing, but not replacing, existing plumbing fixtures" among work exempt from a building permit, and its plumbing permit form charges $65 plus $10 per fixture for existing homes. Ask the office before you buy.

  • EPA WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less. The EPA says the current federal standard is 1.6 gallons, and older toilets can use as much as 6 gallons per flush.

  • Nampa's water report says hard water can leave residue on glassware and plumbing fixtures but is not a public health hazard. The city lists its hardness as ranging from none to 7.08 grains per gallon.

Questions about toilet, faucet and disposal repair

How urgent is a fixture problem?
Begin with a quick 1-to-10 gut check. Up at 8–10, like a spraying supply line, a cracked toilet that leaks or a disposal that smokes, close the main shutoff or keep the disposal off and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__. In the middle (4–7), such as a rocking toilet or water around its base, call this week, because floors rot slowly. Down at 1–3, for a faucet drip or a toilet that runs now and then, jiggle the handle and make sure the tap is fully closed.
Does replacing a toilet or faucet need a permit?
It depends on the city. Nampa's page exempts replacing a broken fixture, while Middleton charges a per-fixture fee on its plumbing permit for existing homes. Ask the building office in your town before you buy, and ask the installer who handles any permit.
Why does my faucet keep clogging with white flakes?
Minerals in hard water can collect in the aerator. The USGS says hardness is not a health concern but can be a nuisance. Nampa publishes a hardness range, and Caldwell's reports do not list one.
What does the WaterSense label on a toilet mean?
The EPA says WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, which is 20 percent less than the 1.6-gallon federal standard.
Does this cover Nampa and Middleton too?
Yes. Fixture help reaches Nampa and Middleton as well. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ and tell them your town.
