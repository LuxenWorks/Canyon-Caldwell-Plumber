Water Softeners in Caldwell, ID

Hardness, softeners and filters in Canyon County

Water softener help in Caldwell is for white crust on faucets, spotted dishes and soap that will not lather. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because scale also builds inside your water heater where you cannot see it.

What you may notice

  • White crust on faucets, showerheads and shower doors
  • Soap that leaves film and does not lather well
  • Spots on dishes and glassware
  • Mineral flakes collecting in aerators
  • A softener that uses salt while the water still feels hard

What is often behind it

  • Hard water, which means water carrying dissolved calcium and magnesium
  • An empty brine tank, the tank that holds the salt used to refresh the softener
  • A resin bed, the small beads that catch the hardness minerals, that has worn out
  • A bypass valve left in the bypass position, so water skips the softener
  • A control head set to the wrong hardness or the wrong schedule

Checks you can make yourself

Stick to checks that feel easy and safe. Never open a water heater, relight a pilot or touch gas piping or wiring; call instead.

  • Read the display or indicator on the softener's control head and write down any message or code.
  • Look at, but do not move, the bypass valve and compare it with the diagram in the owner's manual.
  • Dip a store-bought hardness test strip in cold water from a kitchen tap and compare the result with your utility's published range.
  • Look for water on the floor around the softener, its drain line and its connections.

Stop and phone if

  • Water is leaking from the softener, its drain line or its connections
  • Brine or salty water is spilling onto the floor
  • The unit or its drain is wet near an outlet or wiring
  • Water runs to the drain nonstop

Idaho and city details

  • Idaho Code 54-2602 excepts water treatment installations and repairs from the plumbing certificate requirement when a state agent inspects the workmanship, and it requires anyone doing that work to keep a $2,000 surety bond.

  • The state's homeowner permit worksheet lists "Water conditioning equipment" at $65, and Middleton's plumbing permit lists water conditioners at a $65 basic fee per item. State plumbing rules require a pre-plumbed softener loop in new one- and two-family homes built slab on grade or with a finished basement, and a softener drain line of at least 3/4 inch.

  • Nampa reports hardness from none up to 7.08 grains per gallon. Caldwell's 2025 water quality report lists regulated test results but no hardness figure.

Questions about water softeners and filtration

How serious is hard water or a softener issue?
Begin with a quick 1-to-10 gut check. Up at 8–10, like water leaking from the softener, brine on the floor or a wet unit near wiring, turn off the supply if it is safe and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__. In the middle (4–7), like a softener that burns through salt while the water still feels hard, call within the week. Down at 1–3, light spots on glassware, wipe fixtures and get a hardness number first.
Do Caldwell and Nampa publish their water hardness?
Nampa's 2025 water quality report says hardness ranges from none to 7.08 grains per gallon. Caldwell's 2025 report lists test results for arsenic, nitrate and other substances but gives no hardness figure, so call the water department at (208) 455-3070 or test a tap at home.
Does installing a softener need a permit?
Plan on a permit and an inspection. The state permit worksheet lists a fee for water conditioning equipment, Middleton lists water conditioners on its plumbing permit, and Idaho Code 54-2602 refers to inspection of water treatment work. Ask your town's building office what applies.
Is a softener the same as a filter?
No. A softener addresses hardness minerals. A filter removes particles or other substances, depending on its type. Ask what problem each one is meant to solve before you buy either.
