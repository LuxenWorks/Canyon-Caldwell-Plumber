Plumber in Nampa, ID: heaters, hard water and older pipes

Nampa homes range from new builds to decades-old pipes, and the city's water runs from soft to hard. If a heater is failing, start with water heater repair. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because hard water can wear a tank down.

  1. How old the homes are

    Nampa city has 40,529 housing units and a median build year of 2000, give or take 1 year, according to Census ACS 2020-2024 5-year data (tables B25035 and B25034). The largest group is 11,343 homes (28 percent) built from 2000 to 2009. At the other end, 10,483 homes (26 percent) were built in 1979 or earlier, including 2,639 from 1939 or earlier. A home built from 2000 to 2009 is now between 17 and 26 years old, so original fixtures and appliances may be reaching the point where owners start replacing them, while the older quarter of the stock is where worn supply and drain pipe is most likely.

  2. Valley weather

    NWS Boise sets its thresholds, based on 1991 to 2020 climate data, at 36 degrees F for frost, 32 for a freeze and 28 for a hard freeze. At Boise Airport, the office reports just over 11 inches of rain and melted snow a year, three quarters of it from November through May, plus about 20 inches of snow. It calls freezing precipitation a rare event in the Treasure Valley and says freezing rain and drizzle are more common at the lower end of the valley, near Ontario, Oregon.

  3. Who issues the permit

    Plumbing permits in Nampa come from the City of Nampa Building Department, which works from the Building Safety Division office at 500 12th Avenue South, Nampa, ID 83651, phone (208) 468-5435. The city's page says all plumbing piping and drainage work needs a permit, except leak repairs, broken-fixture replacement and similar emergency repairs. Work must be done by a state-licensed journeyman plumber employed by an Idaho-licensed plumbing contractor, and the page allows owners who will live in the home for 180 days after the work to do it themselves. The city's pages name the 2017 Idaho State Plumbing Code as the standard. The page does not mention water heaters, so ask before replacing one. Contractors who install individual sewer or water service lines wholly outside the building must hold a health-district septic installer certification or a state public works excavator license.

Before you call anyone in Nampa

  • Ask the Building Department before replacing anything beyond a fixture swap. The city exempts leak repairs and broken-fixture replacement, not larger piping or drainage work.

  • Check the hardness range in the city's 2025 water quality report, none to 7.08 grains per gallon, before you buy a softener or decide a heater has failed.

  • If a contractor will dig outside the house, ask which qualification they hold and call 811 first.

  • Before a hard freeze, disconnect hoses and close the inside valve to each outdoor hose bib, leaving the outside bib open, as the Red Cross advises.

  • When your Nampa water heater rumbles, runs out fast or leaves a damp base, call __PHONE_DISPLAY__.

Questions about plumbing in Nampa

  • Does Nampa require a permit for plumbing work?

    Yes for most of it. The city says all plumbing piping and drainage work needs a permit, with exceptions for leak repairs, broken-fixture replacement and similar emergency repairs. The Building Department number is (208) 468-5435.

  • Does Nampa add anything to its water?

    The city's water quality page says chlorine is the only substance it adds and that any fluoride is naturally occurring. Its 2025 report says hard water can leave residue on glassware and fixtures but is not a public health hazard.

  • Who can call from Nampa?

    Homeowners and landlords in Nampa with a plumbing problem can call. Rented apartment or house? The manager's office is your first stop. Get your address and the age of the system ready, then call __PHONE_DISPLAY__.
