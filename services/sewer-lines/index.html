Sewer Line Repair in Caldwell, ID

Buried lines, cleanouts and septic homes

Sewer line repair in Caldwell is for drains that back up together, a wet strip of lawn or a sewage smell in the yard. Pick up the phone and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because the pipe is buried and the first job is finding the break.

What you may notice

  • Several drains slow down or back up together
  • Sewage odor in the yard or near the cleanout (a capped access pipe on the line)
  • A wet, sunken or unusually green strip of lawn above the line
  • Gurgling toilets after the washer runs
  • Water backing up into the lowest tub or floor drain

What is often behind it

  • Roots entering a joint or a crack
  • A cracked, offset or collapsed section of older pipe
  • A sag in the pipe where waste settles and collects
  • Grease, wipes and debris building up over time
  • Damage from earlier digging or heavy loads over the line

Checks you can make yourself

Stick to checks that feel easy and safe. Never open a water heater, relight a pilot or touch gas piping or wiring; call instead.

  • Write down which drains back up first, which follow, and when the trouble started.
  • Walk the yard and look for wet, sunken or unusually green ground along the path of the line.
  • Find the cleanout cap if you can, but do not open it. A backed-up line can release sewage.
  • Phone 811 ahead of any digging, even for a fence post. Marking buried lines is free.

Stop and phone if

  • Sewage is backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain. Stop using water in the house.
  • Sewage is spreading near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  • A sinkhole or sudden low spot opens in the yard near the line.
  • Sewage is surfacing in the yard.

Idaho and city details

  • Idaho Code 54-2602 exempts sewer, excavating and utility contractors from the plumbing certificate when they work on pipe beyond the foundation walls of a building. Nampa asks those contractors to hold either a health-district septic installer certification or a state public works excavator license before applying for a city permit.

  • Digline Idaho says to give at least 2 business days' notice, and no more than 10, before excavation. Homeowners digging on their own property are covered, and marking of buried utility lines is free.

  • Homes outside city sewer service rely on septic. Southwest District Health permits septic tanks and drainfields, and every installer in Idaho must hold a basic or complex installer registration.

Questions about sewer line repair

How urgent is a sewer line problem?
Begin with a quick 1-to-10 gut check. Up at 8–10, like sewage in a tub, near outlets or coming up in the yard, stop using water and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ right away. In the middle (4–7), such as several slow drains or a sunken green strip of lawn, call this week, because a break gets worse. Down at 1–3, an odd smell now and then, check the cleanout cap and watch it.
Who is allowed to repair the sewer line between my house and the street?
Under Idaho Code, sewer and excavating contractors may work beyond the foundation walls without a plumbing certificate, but pipe inside the building falls to certificate holders. Nampa adds its own qualification. Ask for the certificate, registration or license that applies and look it up.
Do I need to call 811 for a sewer repair?
Yes, whenever digging is involved. Digline says excavators must wait until known utility lines are marked, and homeowners are covered. The call is 811, and marking is free.
My home is on septic. Who handles permits?
Southwest District Health permits septic tanks and drainfields and requires a site evaluation before it issues a permit. The district's phone is 208-455-5400.
Does this cover Nampa and Middleton too?
Yes. Sewer help reaches Nampa and Middleton along with Caldwell. Call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ and give your address.
