Leak Repair in Caldwell, ID

Hidden leaks in Canyon County homes

Leak repair in Caldwell starts with the drip you can see, the stain on a wall or the water bill that keeps climbing. Pick up the phone and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__, because water in a wall soaks framing long before a stain shows.

What you may notice

  • A water bill that rises when nobody in the house has changed their habits
  • A soft, stained or bubbling spot on a wall, ceiling or cabinet floor
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off
  • A musty smell that returns after cleaning
  • A water meter dial that keeps turning when all water use inside has stopped

What is often behind it

  • A worn washer, seal or cartridge inside a faucet or shutoff valve (the small valve under a sink or toilet)
  • A cracked or corroded supply line, the pipe or hose that brings pressurized water to a fixture
  • A loose nut or tired gasket at a connection under a sink, behind a toilet or at an appliance
  • A pinhole where an aging metal pipe has worn thin
  • A split fitting or crack left behind by an earlier freeze

Checks you can make yourself

Stick to checks that feel easy and safe. Never open a water heater, relight a pilot or touch gas piping or wiring; call instead.

  • Read the water meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again. The EPA says that if the meter changes at all, you probably have a leak.
  • Look under every sink and behind every toilet for drips, a damp cabinet floor, or crusty white or green buildup on fittings.
  • Put a few drops of food coloring in a toilet tank and wait 10 minutes without flushing. The EPA says color in the bowl means a leak. Flush right away so the tank does not stain.
  • Find the main shutoff valve and make sure every adult in the home knows where it is.

Stop and phone if

  • Water is flowing and no fixture valve will stop it. Close the main shutoff and call.
  • Water is dripping or pooling near outlets, a light fixture or the electrical panel. Keep away from them, close the main shutoff only if you can reach it safely, and call.
  • A ceiling is sagging or bulging, which can mean water is trapped above it.
  • You smell gas near standing water or a damaged appliance. Get everyone outside, then phone the gas utility from there.

Questions about leak repair

How serious is a leak in my house?
Run a quick 1-to-10 gut check. Up at 8–10, with water flowing, a sagging ceiling or water near outlets, close the main shutoff if you can reach it safely and call __PHONE_DISPLAY__ now. In the middle (4–7), like a stain that keeps spreading, a musty smell or a meter dial that spins with everything off, call within the week. Down at 1–3, a dry drip at a fixture, wipe the spot and watch it for a day.
Does fixing a leak in Caldwell, Nampa or Middleton need a permit?
State law exempts work that only fixes a leak or clears a blockage, provided nothing is functionally rearranged. If the repair replaces or reroutes pipe, ask the building office in your town before work starts. Caldwell, Nampa and Middleton each run their own.
How can I tell whether the leak is inside the house?
Read the meter, keep every fixture off for two hours, and read it again. If it moved, close the main shutoff inside the house and watch the meter. If it still moves with that valve closed, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the valve.
Who do I call about water leaking near the meter or the street?
Call your water utility. It can tell you where its responsibility ends and yours begins. Caldwell's water department lists an after-hours emergency line at (208) 250-1638, Middleton lists a Public Works after-hours line at (208) 921-0029, and Nampa's Water and Irrigation Division lists 208-468-5860.
