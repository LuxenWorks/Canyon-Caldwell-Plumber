Hard water in Caldwell and Nampa: scale, water heaters and softeners

Nampa publishes a hardness range for its water, and Caldwell's latest reports do not list one. Here is what hardness means, what it does inside a water heater, and what Idaho requires when a softener goes in.

What hardness is, and what it is not

The U.S. Geological Survey describes hardness as the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium in water. The USGS groups it by milligrams per liter: soft is 0 to 60, moderately hard is 61 to 120, hard is 121 to 180, and very hard is more than 180.

The USGS also says hardness is not a health concern, but it can be a nuisance. Soap reacts with calcium to form scum, so more soap or detergent is needed, and minerals can build up on fixtures and inside equipment.

What Nampa publishes

Nampa's 2025 water quality report says the city's supply comes entirely from 17 groundwater wells, drawn from the western Snake River Plain Aquifer. It states that water hardness "ranges from none to 7.08 grains of hardness per gallon."

The report uses its own grain-per-gallon table: soft is 0 to 1, slightly hard is 1 to 3.5, moderately hard is 3.5 to 7.0, hard is 7.0 to 10.5, and very hard is above 10.5. On that table, Nampa's range runs from soft to the low end of hard. A range means readings differ across the system, so a neighbor's tap may not match yours. The Water and Irrigation Division lists 208-468-5860 for questions.

The city's water quality page adds that chlorine is the only substance Nampa adds to drinking water, and that any fluoride is naturally occurring.

What Caldwell's reports leave out

Caldwell's water comes from 15 groundwater wells across four pressure zones, with two storage tanks holding 2.5 million gallons in total. The city posts annual reports back to 2018 on its water department pages.

The 2025 report lists regulated results such as arsenic at 9.24 parts per billion, nitrate at 2.20 parts per million, and fluoride, and it shows lead and copper sampling from 2023. It gives no hardness figure, and neither did the 2024 or 2023 reports. To learn the hardness at your tap, call the water department at (208) 455-3070, or use a store-bought test strip on a cold kitchen tap.

Homes on a private well are a different case. Southwest District Health says private water supplies are the sole responsibility of the owner, and its Private Water Program offers education, technical help and water sampling for a nominal fee. The district's number is 208-455-5400.

What scale does inside a water heater

The USGS says that when hard water is heated, calcium carbonate deposits can form. It adds that this scale can reduce equipment life, raise heating costs and lower the efficiency of electric water heaters.

In a tank heater, scale and loose sediment settle where the heat is. Popping or rumbling while the tank heats, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, and a heater that takes longer to recover are common clues. They can have other causes too, so describe what you notice to a technician and let them test it.

Flushing or draining a tank involves hot water, and on gas and electric models it involves live connections. Follow the manufacturer's manual, or leave it to a technician if you are unsure. Never remove a cover or panel yourself.

Water heaters, softeners and Idaho permits

Idaho Code 54-2604 includes water heaters, with their dedicated venting, in its definition of a plumbing system. The state's homeowner plumbing permit worksheet, revised in 2022, lists "Water Heater Replacement (only)" at $65 and "Water conditioning equipment" at $65. Middleton's plumbing permit fees list water heaters and water conditioners at a $65 basic fee per item. Caldwell's and Nampa's permit pages do not name either one, so ask the building office in your town.

Idaho Code 54-2602 excepts water treatment installations and repairs from the plumbing certificate requirement when a state agent inspects the workmanship, and it requires a $2,000 surety bond for that work. The state plumbing rules also require new one- and two-family homes built slab on grade, or with a finished basement, to have a pre-plumbed softener loop, and they set a minimum drain line of 3/4 inch for a softener.

A softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium. Filtration is a different tool with a different purpose, so be clear about which problem you are solving.

Questions to settle before you buy

Start with a number. Ask for your utility's hardness range, or test your own tap, so the size of the problem is known before anyone proposes equipment.

Ask whose name goes on the permit and who books the inspection, and get the answer in writing. Ask for the contractor's Idaho certificate number, then check it with the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL) through the license search on the State Plumbing Board page. For water treatment work, ask whether the surety bond Idaho requires is in place.

Finally, ask where the softener's drain line will run and how it will be sized. If your hardness is low, ask whether a softener is needed at all.

When is hard water more than a nuisance?

Cleaning crust off a faucet and flushing a tank are fine on your own. The line is crossed when the heater rumbles, runs cold or leaks, or when scale keeps coming back after cleaning.
